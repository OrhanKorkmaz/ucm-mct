\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent LLM systems communicate through natural language, paying a
decode/re-encode tax in latency and information loss, and---as recent work on
subliminal learning suggests---potentially carrying hidden, model-specific
signals. We present a protocol in which frozen, heterogeneous LLMs exchange
hidden states directly through a jointly learned \emph{neutral hub}: for $N$
models, $2N$ small encoder/decoder ``micro-translators'' ($\sim$1--5M
parameters each) replace $N(N{-}1)$ pairwise bridges. On consumer hardware,
across three independent model families (Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-0.5B,
SmolLM2-360M) and a 10{,}000-concept bilingual benchmark, we show:
(i) translated states preserve identity (up to 88\% top-1 retrieval over a
10k pool) and geometry (Procrustes rank correlation $\rho=0.995$);
(ii) after a tuned-lens-style KL-distillation phase, injected translations are
functionally near-indistinguishable from the receiver's own states (KL ratio
0.17 vs.\ a random control at concept level; 0.016 at sentence level);
(iii) a BatchTopK sparse-autoencoder bridge sustains $8\times$ compression at
a 3.5-point retrieval cost, putting a message at 64--128 bytes;
(iv) the vector channel is ${\sim}900\times$ faster than a text round-trip;
(v) adding a third model family \emph{improves} existing routes (+15 points),
suggesting the shared space is regularized rather than crowded by new members.
We also report a measured limit: relative translation fidelity is
language-dependent (English 1.01---lossless; Turkish 0.39) even though every
model encodes Turkish concepts near-perfectly in isolation, indicating that
the ``Platonic'' shared core is thick where training distributions overlap
and thin elsewhere. Code and benchmarks:
\url{https://github.com/OrhanKorkmaz/ucm-mct}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
LLM agents today cooperate by generating text that other agents re-read.
This channel is expensive---every message pays a full decode and re-encode
cycle---and lossy: graded uncertainty, partial inferences, and other
non-verbalized state do not survive serialization. Prior work has begun to
bypass the token bottleneck: activation-level communication between agents
\cite{ramesh2025activations}, latent-only inter-agent channels
\cite{interlat2026}, state-delta trajectories \cite{sde2025}, and
unsupervised embedding-space translation \cite{jha2025vec2vec}. These efforts
are mostly pairwise: each pair of models needs its own bridge.

We ask a stronger question: can heterogeneous, frozen LLMs share a single
\emph{neutral} representation space that none of them owns---an interlingua
for hidden states---so that joining the network costs one encoder and one
decoder, independent of network size? We call the resulting bridges
\emph{micro cognitive translators} (MCT).

Our contributions: (1) a complete, reproducible protocol (extraction,
CKA-based layer pairing, standardization, translator and hub training,
injection, compression) validated end-to-end on consumer hardware;
(2) the observation that \emph{functional} compatibility does not follow from
vector-space proximity, and a distillation recipe that reliably closes the
gap at both concept and sentence level; (3) a positive scaling result for the
neutral hub (a third family improves existing routes); and (4) a measured,
language-dependent limit of cross-model representational overlap that
constrains any interlingua approach.

\section{Method}
\paragraph{Micro cognitive translators.} An MCT maps source states
$x \in \mathbb{R}^{d_A}$ to target states in $\mathbb{R}^{d_B}$:
\[
y = \mu_{\text{out}} + s\,W(x-\mu_{\text{in}}) + g\cdot\mathrm{MLP}(x-\mu_{\text{in}}),
\]
with $W$ initialized by orthogonal Procrustes on training pairs and kept
semi-orthogonal by a soft penalty $\lVert W^\top W - I\rVert^2$ (hard
orthogonality parametrizations were numerically unstable in our setting);
$g$ gates a residual MLP that starts at zero. All training operates on
per-dimension z-standardized activations---indispensable in practice because
massive-activation dimensions otherwise dominate every objective.

\paragraph{Neutral hub.} Each model $m$ contributes spokes (selected layers)
with an encoder $E_{m}$ into a shared $d_h$-dimensional space and a decoder
$D_{m}$ back. The hub is trained jointly with symmetric InfoNCE across all
spoke pairs, reconstruction, cross-translation anchors, and vec2vec-style
cycle consistency ($A\!\to\!\text{hub}\!\to\!B\!\to\!\text{hub}\!\to\!A$).
No pairwise translators are trained; every route goes through the hub.

\paragraph{Functional alignment by distillation.} Vector-space training
alone produces translations that are \emph{close} (cosine $\approx$ 0.73)
yet \emph{functionally destructive} when injected: the residual error is
out-of-distribution for the receiver's downstream layers. We therefore add a
tuned-lens-style phase: the translated vector is injected into the frozen
receiver at the target layer (single position, norm-calibrated), and the KL
divergence between the resulting next-token distribution and the receiver's
clean distribution is minimized, with gradients flowing only into the
translator. This phase moved concept-level injection agreement from 27\% to
81\%, and sentence-level agreement from 27\% to 83\% (KL ratio 0.016).

\paragraph{Compression.} Messages are hub vectors passed through a BatchTopK
sparse autoencoder with an auxiliary dead-feature revival loss; only the
$(index, value)$ pairs of the $k$ active features travel.

\section{Experimental setup}
Models: Llama-3.2-1B (16 layers, $d=2048$), Qwen2.5-0.5B (24 layers,
$d=896$), SmolLM2-360M (32 layers, $d=960$); all frozen, bfloat16, Apple M2
(16 GB). Benchmark: 10{,}000 frequency-ranked concepts (8{,}000 English,
2{,}000 Turkish), 80/20 split; contextual last-token states under
concept-final templates; layer pairs selected by linear CKA within relative
depth bands. Injection protocol: three conditions (clean / translated /
random real vector), single-position replacement with norm calibration.

\paragraph{Scaling behavior.} With two families, the semantic route
Llama$\to$Qwen reached 72.5\% top-1 through the hub. Adding SmolLM2-360M
(two new translators only) raised it to 87.7\%, and all six cross-family
routes landed at 79--88\%. New members appear to act as regularizers of the
shared space, analogously to multilingual training.

\paragraph{The relative-fidelity limit.} We measure each model's
\emph{self-consistency ceiling} (retrieving a concept encoded under one
template from a pool encoded under another; $\geq$0.98 for both languages in
all models) and compare cross-model translation against it. English:
0.988/0.979 = 1.01---the channel is lossless. Turkish: 0.387/0.988 = 0.39.
Both libraries know Turkish; their Turkish \emph{geometries} do not align.
Functional distillation at sentence level narrows the Turkish gap
substantially (72\% vs.\ 86\% agreement), indicating that functional
alignment is easier than identity alignment where the shared core is thin.

\paragraph{Robustness.} Simulated representation drift (mixing 40\% of an
adjacent layer's geometry plus noise) cost only $\sim$1 point through the
hub; a 500-concept canary set with closed-form Procrustes repair restores
performance when degradation does occur, and should be triggered only below
a degradation threshold---premature recalibration on small canaries hurts.

\section{Related work}
Model stitching \cite{lenc2015,bansal2021}; activation communication between
agents \cite{ramesh2025activations}; latent-only communication
\cite{interlat2026}; state-delta encoding \cite{sde2025}; unsupervised
embedding translation \cite{jha2025vec2vec}; tuned lens \cite{belrose2023};
crosscoders and model diffing \cite{anthropic2025crosscoders}; subliminal
learning \cite{cloud2025subliminal}; introspective detection of injected
concepts \cite{lindsey2025introspection}. Our work differs in the N-model
neutral-hub formulation, the cross-model depth translation, the
distillation-based functional alignment recipe, and the measured
language-dependence of the shared core.

\section{Limitations and safety}
All models are $\leq$1B parameters; scale generalization is untested.
Sentence-level results use template-generated contexts, not free text.
The receiver could not name injected concepts (0\% at 0.5B), consistent with
introspection emerging at scale. A vector channel is a new attack surface;
we propose (and will evaluate) an SAE-based firewall that passes shared
semantic features and drops model-exclusive components---the same mechanism
that makes the channel auditable makes it defensible.

\section{Conclusion}
A neutral interlingua for LLM hidden states is buildable today, on a laptop:
identity, geometry, function, compression, and speed all survive
translation, and the hub improves as families join. The measured thinness of
the shared core outside high-resource language regions is, to our knowledge,
the first quantitative boundary drawn for this family of approaches---and a
concrete target for making machine communication genuinely universal.
